The Ivy League is not the elite MBA set

The Ivy League is an athletic conference of eight universities, and it maps badly onto business schools. Two Ivies have no MBA at all. Two more have MBAs that sit outside the top ten. Meanwhile the four schools most responsible for producing founders — Stanford, MIT Sloan, Chicago Booth, Berkeley Haas — are not Ivy League and never were.

The set you actually mean is the M7: Harvard, Stanford, Wharton, Chicago Booth, Northwestern Kellogg, MIT Sloan, Columbia. Three of those seven are Ivy. Applying "Ivy League only" as a filter would make you skip Stanford GSB, which sends roughly 16% of its class straight into starting companies — the highest of any elite programme, and the single school that best matches the reason you gave for going abroad.

So the filter is M7 plus Berkeley Haas, plus Yale and IESE for structural reasons. Everything below that line is correctly excluded, and this page holds that line. The full Ivy roster is audited immediately below so you can see exactly what you are and are not applying to.

"Ivy League" is a sports conference, not a quality tier

Eight universities. Only six issue an MBA at all: Harvard, Wharton (Penn), Columbia, Yale SOM, Cornell Johnson, Dartmouth Tuck. Brown has no business school, only a 15-month executive MBA run jointly with IE Madrid for senior managers with 10+ years of experience. Princeton has no MBA of any kind.

Meanwhile Stanford GSB, MIT Sloan, Chicago Booth and Berkeley Haas are not Ivy League — and Stanford and MIT sit above four of the six Ivy MBAs on every founder metric that exists. Cutting Stanford from your list because Dartmouth plays in a different football conference would be the single most expensive mistake available to you here.

The tier you actually mean is M7: Harvard, Stanford, Wharton, Booth, Kellogg, MIT Sloan, Columbia. Only three of those are Ivy. Every Ivy MBA is now tracked below regardless, so the union of "all Ivy" and "all M7" is covered and nothing gets dropped on a technicality.
